AbstractResumo (original, em inglês)

Renewable-energy planning depends on reliable solar-irradiation forecasts, yet measurement infrastructure remains sparse across much of Brazil, leaving practitioners with abundant data at a few well-instrumented sites and almost nothing elsewhere. This paper addresses that scarcity through a neurosymbolic (NSAI) approach: rather than treating transfer learning as a purely statistical operation, I propose to embed a well-founded domain ontology inside the decision procedure that should govern whether a model trained at a data-rich source site may be deployed at a data-scarce target site, and if so, how much local data the target needs before adaptation is warranted. The central question is partly conceptual and partly empirical: when can a model trained in one context be trusted in another, and how much local evidence is needed before that trust is justified? Within this paradigm, symbolic, connectionist, and statistical components are combined; the distinguishing choice here is to ground the symbolic component in a well-founded domain ontology, which would formalize domain knowledge about site similarity (climate type, biome, geography, radiative regime) so that ontological commitments become explicit, inspectable, and testable. To this end, I am developing a Neurosymbolic Semantic Gate: a symbolic decision layer that will operationalize these commitments to govern transfer. This statement lays out the motivation, the core ideas, and the experimental program: a principled investigation of when ontological structure can govern transfer, and of how much local data a target site truly needs.
